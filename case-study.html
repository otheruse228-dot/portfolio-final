Running a business on chat

The problem

The work already happened in chat. Technicians reported faults as voice notes in WhatsApp groups. Staff typed payment updates and daily route lists in Telegram. Managers asked for stock, service and payment status whenever they needed it.

Turning all of that into clean data meant someone had to listen, retype, chase approvals and build reports by hand. The goal: keep people in the chat apps they already use, and let the systems do the typing, checking and reporting.

How the system fits together

Messages come in from the chat apps, the automation layer understands them, and everything lands in Google Sheets, which stay the single source of truth.

FLOW 1 · WHATSAPP

Voice note to complaint ticket

  1. A technician sends a voice note in the service group.
  2. The voice note is transcribed.
  3. The text is split into separate complaints for the same generator.
  4. Each complaint gets a category (Engine, Electrical) and an urgency.
  5. It is logged with who sent it and when, and a confirmation goes back to the group.

FLOW 2 · WHATSAPP

Purchase rate approval

  1. A purchase entry is made with vendor quotes.
  2. The bot compares the quotes and marks the cheaper one.
  3. The manager decides with one tap on WhatsApp.
  4. If the buttons do not show on a phone, a short reply code such as “OK 48B20” works too.

FLOW 3 · TELEGRAM

Payment follow-ups and routes

  1. Staff type an update in plain words, like “payment 12 Oct ko hogi”.
  2. The bot finds the client in the master list.
  3. It updates the status, note and follow-up date, and replies with who updated it and when.
  4. For daily route lists, any site name it cannot match is flagged for manual assignment instead of being guessed.

FLOW 4 · CLAUDE + MCP

Ask the business a question

  1. A manager asks in plain words, for example “kaunse parts minimum stock se kam hain?”
  2. Claude picks the right tool from the MCP server, here get_ims_dashboard.
  3. The tool reads the live inventory sheet.
  4. The answer comes back with the numbers: 45 parts checked, 13 need attention, biggest gaps first.

Hard problems I solved

Names never match

Staff type the same site name in many spellings. Names are matched against the master list, and anything uncertain is flagged for a person instead of guessed.

Buttons do not always show

WhatsApp buttons fail on some phones. Every approval also accepts a short reply code, so it works for everyone.

One voice note, many problems

Technicians report several faults in one breath. The bot splits them into separate tickets, each with its own category and urgency.

The AI must follow business rules

Rules live in the MCP tool descriptions. Example: “low stock” means strictly below minimum, and items exactly at minimum are left out unless someone asks for them.

Bad readings, bad reports

Meter readings that jump or drop suddenly are flagged. One vehicle showing 375 km/kg was traced to an odometer entry mistake, not real mileage.

Reading versus changing data

Reporting tools only read. Entry tools (add, submit, update) are separate, so it is always clear which actions change the sheets.

What changed

  • Updates go from the chat straight into the sheets, without retyping.
  • Rate approvals happen with one tap on WhatsApp instead of phone calls.
  • Low-stock lists and payment reminders go out automatically.
  • Managers get answers by asking, instead of waiting for someone to build a report.
